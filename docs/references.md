# References

What good looks like. Each rendered piece names its closest reference in `QUALITY_LOG.md`, and the one-line gap against it.

## Motion and math

- 3Blue1Brown, "Essence of linear algebra" chapter 3 (linear transformations): the grid deformation. Faint background grid, a brighter transformed grid, basis vectors in two colors, labels that stay upright.
- 3Blue1Brown, "But what is a Fourier series?": epicycles. Thin circles at low opacity, a bright traced path, the drawing tip as the focal point.
- 3Blue1Brown, "The essence of calculus" chapter 1: Riemann rectangles with thin outlines, a single fill color, the area number counting up in the corner.
- 3Blue1Brown, "Grover's algorithm" (2025): amplitude bars in a row, the marked bar in the accent color, the reflection geometry drawn as a 2D plane with two basis directions.
- Manim Community showcase, "Moving Frame Box" and "Sine and cosine curves": tight label placement, color by token in TeX.

## Static math and figures

- Knuth, "Concrete Mathematics", typeset in Concrete and Euler: figure line weights and the space around displayed equations.
- Nielsen and Chuang, "Quantum Computation and Quantum Information", circuit figures: thin wires, square gates with centered labels, filled control dots with a thin vertical line, meter glyph for measurement, double lines for classical wires.
- Nature and Physical Review figure style: 7 to 9 pt sans labels, thin axes, no box around the plot, ticks outside.

## UI

- Linear app, issue view: one neutral background, hairline dividers, 13 px UI text, accent only on the active element.
- Vercel docs: left nav in muted ink, content column at about 70 characters, code blocks with a subtle surface, no shadows.
- Observable notebook: cells as plain blocks, output first, code second.
- Figma editor: left layer panel, right property inspector, canvas in the middle with neutral gray surround, 11 to 12 px panel text.

## Board styles

- Chalk lecture photos (for example, Richard Feynman's blackboard at Caltech): chalk strokes are broken, grainy, brighter at the start of a stroke, and slightly wider than a pen. The board has faint erased ghosting.
- Whiteboard lecture recordings (MIT OCW 18.06 recitations): marker lines are uniform with slight darkening where strokes overlap, colors are the standard marker set (black, blue, red, green) but muted by the white glare.
- Engineering blueprints (cyanotype prints): Prussian blue ground with a faint paper texture, white line work of one or two weights, dimension lines with small arrowheads and centered numbers.
